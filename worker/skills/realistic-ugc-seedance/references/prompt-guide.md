## 1. Structure
Write the prompt in English, in this order:

```
[FORMAT] Ultra-realistic vertical 9:16 iPhone UGC video, [N] seconds, filmed handheld by a real person. Looks like an authentic social media post, not an ad.

[PRODUCT] The product is exactly the one in the reference images: [name], [exact colors, materials, size relative to hands/body, logo placement, distinctive parts]. Keep its shape, colors, branding and proportions identical in every shot.

[SETTING] [Specific location — type of home/room, furniture, decor, mess level, what's on walls/tables]. [Time of day, weather outside the window, season].
[LIGHTING] [Light sources — window daylight from left, warm lamp, ceiling lights, fireplace glow], [color temperature], natural shadows.

[PEOPLE] [Each person: age, build, hair, clothing with colors, vibe]. Ordinary-looking real people, natural skin texture, no makeup look.

[SHOTS]
0–3s: [framing + camera move] — [action] — [emotion on faces] — [dialogue in quotes, who says it] — [sounds]
3–6s: ...
(one line per shot, until the end)

[AUDIO] Natural on-camera audio recorded by the phone: [list of sounds]. Spoken dialogue in English, perfectly lip-synced. No background music, no voiceover narrator.

[RULES] No text, no captions, no subtitles, no logos other than on the product, no graphic overlays. Realistic physics and hand interaction. Slight handheld shake, iPhone auto-exposure, natural motion blur.
```

## 2. Realism rules (what made the reference clips look real)
- **Ordinary everything.** Real lived-in homes (a blanket on the couch, mugs on the table, a deer head on the wall, a dog lying around), normal clothes (hoodies, flannel, camo, sweatpants), regular-looking people. Polished = AI.
- **Phone camera, not cinema.** Handheld, slight shake, auto-focus hunting, auto-exposure shifts when moving to a window, wide iPhone lens, sometimes a bit off-center. Never "cinematic", "4K", "dramatic lighting", "bokeh", "studio".
- **Specific light.** Name the light source and direction. Mixed warm/cool household light reads as real.
- **Real emotion beats.** Genuine micro-reactions: double-take, jaw drop, laughing, kid jumping, high-five, hug, "no way". Describe the face and body, not just "happy".
- **Small imperfections.** Someone half out of frame, a hand blocking the lens for a moment, a pet walking through, background noise.
- **Short shots.** 2–4 seconds each. Long single shots give the model time to drift.

## 3. Product fidelity
- Always say "exactly the product in the reference images" and then describe it anyway — colors, materials, logo, size vs. a hand.
- Show the product clearly at least twice, well lit, not tiny in frame.
- Keep hand interactions simple: holding, pressing a big button, opening a box, placing it down. Avoid fine manipulation of tiny parts.
- If there's packaging, describe the box (color, where the product image/logo is) and attach a box photo.

## 4. Dialogue & sound
- Dialogue in quotes, attributed to a person, short and natural (people say "wait what", "no way dude", "this is actually sick"). Max ~1 short line per 2–3 seconds. Overlapping lines and long sentences break lip-sync.
- Say "spoken in English, lip-synced".
- List the real-life sounds per shot: cardboard tearing, plastic wrap crinkle, box flaps, footsteps on wood, couch creak, product clicks/beeps/motor whir, laughter, gasps, dog barking, TV in the background.
- No music (the user adds trending sound later) and no narrator unless asked.

## 5. Shot pacing
- **0–2s = hook.** The most surprising or emotional moment, or a pattern-interrupt. No slow intros.
- Middle = show the product doing its thing from 2–3 angles.
- Last 2–3s = payoff (reaction, result, emotional moment).
- 15s ≈ 5–6 shots; 30s ≈ 9–11 shots.

## 6. What breaks (avoid)
- Readable text on the product other than the main logo, screens with lots of small UI text.
- Many people talking at once; more than 3–4 characters.
- Complex physics (pouring, liquids splashing in detail, fast sports), fingers doing intricate things.
- Words like "cinematic", "epic", "hyperrealistic 8K", "commercial", "advertisement".
- Contradictions between shots (person's shirt color changing) — keep wardrobe identical and restate it if needed.

## 7. Full example (gift reaction, 15s)

```
Ultra-realistic vertical 9:16 iPhone UGC video, 15 seconds, filmed handheld by a mom standing in the living room. Looks like an authentic family video posted on TikTok, not an ad.

The product is exactly the one in the reference images: a camo-patterned projector hunting game set with two orange-tipped toy rifles and a dome-shaped camo projector with an orange top light, packed in a dark green box with the product photo on the front. Keep its shape, colors, branding and proportions identical in every shot.

Setting: a cozy cabin-style living room in the evening — knotty pine wood walls, a mounted deer head above a dark brown leather couch, plaid throw blanket, a framed lake painting, beige carpet. Winter, dark outside the window. Lighting: warm table lamps and overhead ceiling light, slightly yellow, soft natural shadows.

People: dad, early 30s, stocky, ginger beard, camo jacket over a grey t-shirt. Two boys: older boy about 10, short brown hair, grey hoodie and grey sweatpants; younger boy about 7, blond, red henley shirt and jeans. Ordinary real people, natural skin texture.

0–3s: medium shot, slight handheld shake — both boys sit on the couch staring at a tablet and a phone; dad walks in from the left, grabs the tablet and phone out of their hands. Boys look up annoyed. Older boy: "Hey!" Sound: footsteps, couch creak.
3–5s: dad pulls the box from behind his back and holds it up. Both boys' jaws drop, the younger one jumps to his feet. Younger boy: "No way!" Sound: excited gasps.
5–8s: close-up from above on a wooden coffee table — hands rip open the box, revealing the two rifles and the projector in foam. Sound: cardboard tearing, plastic crinkle.
8–10s: low angle on the carpet — dad places the projector on the floor and presses the orange top button; a beam of light shoots to the wall. Sound: click, soft electronic beep.
10–13s: from behind the older boy — he aims the toy rifle at a bright forest hunting game projected big on the wall, fires, and jumps with joy. Older boy: "I got it!" Sound: toy rifle click, game hit sound, laughter.
13–15s: dad and the older boy turn to each other and high-five, both laughing. Dad: "Best Christmas ever." Sound: laughter, high-five clap.

Natural on-camera audio recorded by the phone with slight room echo. Spoken dialogue in English, perfectly lip-synced. No background music, no narrator.

No text, no captions, no subtitles, no graphic overlays. Realistic physics and hand interaction. Slight handheld shake, iPhone auto-exposure, natural motion blur.
```
