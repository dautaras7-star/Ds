# Improving short-form visuals

Use this for visual-upgrade briefs, including Esteban's default recast workflow. The aim is stronger visual communication and credible short-form appeal relative to the source. Actual retention, sharing, sales or virality remain outcomes to measure.

## Diagnose before choosing references

Inspect the opening frame and first few seconds, the reaction, the first clear product demonstration and each setting change. At phone viewing size, identify what attracts attention and what competes with it. Briefly inspect without relying on captions or sound: is the action or curiosity still understandable? Apply the requested voice policy while preserving the script and agreed music/effects in production; this silent inspection is only a visual check.

Write a compact improvement brief: product and use context; platform and objective if known; strongest part of the source; weakest visual moment; locked elements; and the few changes most likely to help. Infer reasonable context from the supplied reel and existing brief, label it when uncertain, and continue without an unnecessary questionnaire. Do not force an improvement where the source already does the job well.

For a faithful recast, keep actions, camera geometry, shot order, cut times and product details locked. Preserve the script and agreed music/effects; replace the speaker when the requested presenter/voice calls for it, following [format-voice-identity.md](format-voice-identity.md). Improve the scene treatment around them. For an explicitly requested relocation, follow [location-adaptation.md](location-adaptation.md): translate furnishings/supports and related B-roll to the new setting while retaining essential interaction geometry and the edit. If the weak hook requires a new action or a different edit, identify that as an optional creative variant rather than quietly rewriting the source.

## Learn from relevant examples

Use the user's accepted work and benchmark first, then inspect a small useful set of current comparable videos. Typically three to five is enough to form an initial hypothesis; expand only to resolve a specific uncertainty. Match product/use context, platform, format and objective before copying a trend. Prefer recent examples when discussing what is currently popular, record dates, and label older examples rather than presenting them as current trends. Do not browse indefinitely in pursuit of an exhaustive viral database.

- Use actual Reels, TikToks or Shorts for motion, hook and performance observations. Watch the relevant portions when accessible; a thumbnail is only a still reference.
- TikTok Creative Center Top Ads can supply paid-ad examples. Its auction-ad selection is not evidence of organic virality. An ad appearing in a public ad library does not establish that it is profitable.
- Use Pinterest for the requested production photographs and visual discovery. A pin's saves, ranking or attractive setting do not establish video retention or sales.
- Use the user's own analytics when supplied. If access or metrics are missing, keep working from inspected visual evidence and label the performance rationale as a hypothesis. Never invent results or treat a login wall as completed research.

For each useful benchmark, record its URL, platform, publication/retrieval dates, format, product relevance, paid/organic/unknown status, inspected timestamp, visible visual mechanism and available metrics. Distinguish visible public views/likes from private retention or conversion data. Compare similar durations and distribution conditions where possible; a large creator, paid reach or older upload can explain larger totals. Repeated patterns across examples support a testable idea, not a causal claim.

Translate the evidence into a concrete mechanism: for example, a face and held product remain clear against quiet shelving, an existing reveal becomes readable because the display is uncluttered, or a material close-up has enough light to show texture. Avoid unsupported statements such as “red backgrounds go viral” or “this avatar will convert.”

## Choose upgrades that fit the shot

These are art-direction heuristics to test, not universal ranking signals:

| Dimension | Useful improvement | Reject or preserve instead when… |
|---|---|---|
| Opening-frame clarity | Clear focal point, visible face/gesture, readable context and space around the existing action | The new setting steals attention, hides the hook or needs a different action |
| Reaction readability | Natural skin texture, suitable light, clear eyes/mouth and separation from the background | Glamour styling, filters or identity changes weaken the accepted creator presentation |
| Product visibility | Contrast against the setting, visible texture and unobstructed hand contact | Styling changes packaging, shape, color, controls, benefits or physical use |
| Background relevance | A believable place to discover, gift or use this specific product | Luxury scenery or novelty has no connection to the action or audience context |
| Light and color | Consistent light direction, useful depth and a restrained palette that separates face and product | Saturation, reflections or dramatic light make the footage feel synthetic |
| Change across shots | Existing cuts reveal distinct but coherent use contexts; each view adds readable information | A “pattern interrupt” invents cuts, flashes, unrelated scenery or early background changes |
| Mobile composition | Face, hands and product remain legible and clear of platform interface areas | A crop loses the product/action or breaks locked framing; never silently crop to solve it |

Search Pinterest from the desired improvement plus the source geometry. “Bright retail aisle with a clear center path and quiet shelves behind the face” is more useful than “viral background.” Select the actual photograph, then judge how well it can support the camera height, subject scale, contact surfaces and direction of movement. Reuse the identity the user approved when the task is a refinement; do not recast again just to manufacture novelty.

For the beige cape example, a darker, quieter area behind the beige fabric may improve its silhouette, and soft natural light can make the fleece texture and button action easier to see. Keep the stadium until the real cut to pumpkins. These are visual hypotheses for this product, not a mandate to darken every background or add an earlier product reveal.

## Make and verify the improvement

For each selected change, record:

`shot/time → observed weakness → proposed treatment → intended benefit → benchmark/evidence or hypothesis → reference ID → constraint/tradeoff → rendered verdict`

Choose one coherent direction with a few high-value changes rather than a pile of unrelated trends. Keep a second direction only if it tests a meaningful uncertainty. Generate variants only within the requested scope and allowance; an internal comparison does not require extra paid outputs or a new approval gate.

After rendering, compare source and output at matched opening, reaction, demonstration and cut-adjacent frames. Mark each proposed gain **better / similar / worse** with a short visible reason. These are comparative judgments, not a numeric viral score. Reject regressions in realism, face consistency, product accuracy, physical contact, text policy or edit continuity even when a background looks attractive. The final footage must show the gain; a better reference image alone is insufficient.

Keep Esteban's text-free rule. Platform advice about captions, stickers or graphics does not override it. Make the visual action understandable through composition and scene choice, and flag a text-dependent hook that cannot be repaired within locked actions instead of silently adding words.

## Learn from results

Keep brief dated project notes distinguishing user taste, visual QA and measured audience response. With user-provided or authorized analytics, record the version, change tested, platform, duration, reporting window, audience/distribution and metric definitions. For Shorts, “stayed to watch,” engaged views and average percentage viewed answer different questions. For other platforms use their current available retention, watch-time, completion or share measures without pretending definitions are interchangeable. Sales-oriented paid creative needs the campaign's conversion/cost measures as well as attention metrics.

Compare against the source or a relevant account baseline under comparable conditions. Change one main visual hypothesis per test when attribution matters. Note confounders and revise the heuristic when evidence disagrees. Stronger views alone do not prove stronger sales. Do not publish, run ads, schedule monitoring or spend on a test merely to complete this learning loop.

## Reference basis

Checked September 24, 2026. Refresh relevant guidance and real examples when a current trend or platform constraint matters; these sources do not certify a particular reference as viral.

- [TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en): recommends platform-native vertical video, clear early hooks, people and a less overproduced presentation; frames these as starting points to test. Its caption/graphics suggestions do not override a text-free brief.
- [TikTok Top Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/inspiration/topads/pc/en): a source of auction-ad examples with relevance filters, not an organic performance database. Availability can depend on access; inspect returned examples before claiming findings.
- [Meta Reels ads guidance](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads): supports vertical video, sound and keeping key elements within interface-safe areas. Its cited paid-ad comparisons do not establish organic virality or a universal visual recipe.
- [YouTube content performance definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en-uk): defines Shorts engaged views, stayed-to-watch and watch-duration/percentage measures. Use the definitions when interpreting supplied results rather than equating raw public views with retention.
